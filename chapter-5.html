5.1 Ratios and Rates

💡 The big idea

A ratio compares two quantities, like 3 cats to 5 dogs, written 3 : 5 or 3/5. A rate is a special ratio comparing amounts measured in different units, such as 120 miles in 2 hours. A unit rate tells you how much for exactly one unit, so 120 miles in 2 hours is 60 miles per hour. Ratios can even use fractions, like 1/2 cup of sugar per 3/4 cup of flour, and you find the unit rate by dividing.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • ratio: a comparison of two quantities
  • rate: a ratio comparing amounts measured in different units
  • unit rate: a rate that compares to 1 unit
  • complex fraction: a fraction with a fraction in its numerator, denominator, or both

✏️ Worked example

Problem: You walk 3/4 mile in 1/4 hour. What is your speed in miles per hour?

  1. Unit rate = (3/4) ÷ (1/4).
  2. = 3/4 × 4/1 = 3

Answer: 3 miles per hour

🎯 Practice

  1. Write the ratio 12 boys to 18 girls in simplest form.

    Answer: 2 : 3

  2. A car uses 9 gallons of gas to go 270 miles. Find the unit rate in miles per gallon.

    Answer: 30 miles per gallon

  3. Which is the better buy: 6 juice boxes for $4.20 or 10 for $6.50?

    Answer: 10 for $6.50 ($0.65 each vs. $0.70 each)

  4. A recipe uses 2/3 cup of milk for every 1/2 cup of oats. How many cups of milk per cup of oats?

    Answer: 1 1/3 cups of milk per cup of oats

  5. A snail crawls 5/8 foot in 1/4 minute. What is its speed in feet per minute?

    Answer: 2 1/2 feet per minute

5.2 Proportions

💡 The big idea

A proportion is an equation that says two ratios are equal, like 2/3 = 8/12. Two quantities are proportional if their ratios are always the same. To test whether two ratios form a proportion, simplify both or compare their cross products: 2 × 12 = 24 and 3 × 8 = 24, so 2/3 = 8/12 is a proportion. You can also look in a table to see if every pair has the same unit rate.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • proportion: an equation stating two ratios are equal
  • proportional: having a constant ratio
  • cross products: in a/b = c/d, the products ad and bc

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Do 4/10 and 6/15 form a proportion?

  1. Cross products: 4 × 15 = 60 and 10 × 6 = 60.
  2. They are equal.

Answer: Yes, they form a proportion.

🎯 Practice

  1. Do 3/5 and 9/15 form a proportion?

    Answer: Yes (3 × 15 = 45 and 5 × 9 = 45)

  2. Do 7/8 and 5/6 form a proportion?

    Answer: No (7 × 6 = 42 but 8 × 5 = 40)

  3. A table shows 2 hours → $18 and 5 hours → $45. Is pay proportional to time?

    Answer: Yes, both are $9 per hour.

  4. Do 1.2/4 and 3/10 form a proportion?

    Answer: Yes (1.2 × 10 = 12 and 4 × 3 = 12)

  5. Is the ratio 4 laps : 6 minutes proportional to 10 laps : 16 minutes?

    Answer: No (4/6 ≈ 0.67 lap per minute but 10/16 = 0.625)

Extension 5.2 Graphing Proportional Relationships

💡 The big idea

When two quantities are proportional, their graph is a straight line through the origin (0, 0). The point (1, r) on that line shows the unit rate r, also called the constant of proportionality. If a graph is curved or does not go through (0, 0), the relationship is not proportional. You can read the unit rate right off the graph by finding y when x = 1.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • origin: the point (0, 0)
  • constant of proportionality: the unit rate r in y = rx

✏️ Worked example

Problem: The points (0, 0), (2, 7), and (4, 14) are on a line. Is it proportional, and what is the unit rate?

  1. The line passes through (0, 0).
  2. y ÷ x: 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5 and 14 ÷ 4 = 3.5.

Answer: Yes; the unit rate is 3.5, so the point (1, 3.5) is on the line.

🎯 Practice

  1. Do the points (1, 4), (2, 8), and (3, 12) show a proportional relationship?

    Answer: Yes, y ÷ x is always 4 and the line goes through (0, 0).

  2. Do the points (0, 2), (1, 5), and (2, 8) show a proportional relationship?

    Answer: No. When x = 0, y is 2, so the graph misses the origin. Also, y ÷ x is not constant (5 ÷ 1 = 5 but 8 ÷ 2 = 4).

  3. A line through the origin passes through (5, 30). What is the unit rate?

    Answer: 6

  4. A proportional graph has unit rate 2.5. What is y when x = 8?

    Answer: y = 20

  5. A line through the origin passes through (4, 3). Which point (1, r) is on it?

    Answer: (1, 0.75)

5.3 Writing Proportions

💡 The big idea

To write a proportion for a word problem, set up two ratios that compare the same things in the same order. For example, if 3 notebooks cost $5, then 3/5 = 12/x compares notebooks to dollars on both sides. You can also use a ratio table to organize the matching quantities. Keeping the units in the same places (top and bottom) is the key to setting it up correctly.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • proportion: two equal ratios
  • ratio table: a table of equivalent ratios
  • unknown: the variable you are solving for

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Write and solve a proportion: 3 notebooks cost $5. How much do 12 notebooks cost?

  1. notebooks/dollars: 3/5 = 12/x.
  2. 12 is 4 times 3, so x is 4 times 5.
  3. x = 20

Answer: $20

🎯 Practice

  1. Write a proportion: 4 cups of flour make 2 dozen cookies. How many cups for 5 dozen? (Use c.)

    Answer: 4/2 = c/5, so c = 10 cups

  2. A map scale says 2 cm = 15 km. Write a proportion for 7 cm and solve it.

    Answer: 2/15 = 7/d, so d = 52.5 km

  3. You read 18 pages in 30 minutes. At that rate, how many pages in 50 minutes?

    Answer: 30 pages (18/30 = p/50)

  4. Complete the ratio table: 3 → 7, 6 → ?, 15 → ?

    Answer: 6 → 14 and 15 → 35

  5. A school has 5 teachers for every 120 students. How many teachers for 840 students?

    Answer: 35 teachers

5.4 Solving Proportions

💡 The big idea

There are a few ways to solve a proportion. You can use mental math with equivalent ratios, use the Multiplication Property of Equality to get the variable alone, or use the Cross Products Property. With cross products, a/b = c/d becomes ad = bc, and then you solve that equation. Always check that your answer makes sense in the problem.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • Cross Products Property: if a/b = c/d, then ad = bc
  • solve a proportion: find the value of the variable that makes the ratios equal

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Solve 6/x = 9/12.

  1. Cross products: 9x = 6 × 12 = 72.
  2. Divide by 9: x = 8.

Answer: x = 8

🎯 Practice

  1. Solve x/4 = 15/20.

    Answer: x = 3

  2. Solve 5/8 = 20/y.

    Answer: y = 32

  3. Solve 7/n = 2.8/4.

    Answer: n = 10

  4. Solve (k + 2)/3 = 10/6.

    Answer: k = 3

  5. Laila earns $42 for 6 hours of work. How much for 15 hours?

    Answer: $105

5.5 Slope

💡 The big idea

The slope of a line tells how steep it is. It is the rise (vertical change) divided by the run (horizontal change) between any two points. Lines going up from left to right have a positive slope, and lines going down have a negative slope. When a line shows a rate, like dollars per hour, the slope is that rate of change.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • slope: rise ÷ run
  • rise: the vertical change
  • run: the horizontal change

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the slope of the line through (1, 2) and (5, 10).

  1. Rise = 10 − 2 = 8.
  2. Run = 5 − 1 = 4.
  3. Slope = 8 ÷ 4 = 2

Answer: 2

🎯 Practice

  1. Find the slope through (0, 0) and (3, 12).

    Answer: 4

  2. Find the slope through (2, 5) and (6, 7).

    Answer: 1/2

  3. Find the slope through (−1, 4) and (3, −4).

    Answer: −2

  4. A line rises 9 units over a run of 6 units. What is the slope?

    Answer: 3/2

  5. A pool fills from 0 gallons to 450 gallons in 30 minutes at a steady rate. What is the slope (rate of change)?

    Answer: 15 gallons per minute

5.6 Direct Variation

💡 The big idea

Two quantities show direct variation when y = kx for some nonzero number k. The number k is the constant of proportionality, and it is also the slope of the graph. The graph of a direct variation is a straight line through the origin. To find k, divide y by x for any point that is not the origin.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • direct variation: a relationship y = kx
  • constant of proportionality: the number k in y = kx

✏️ Worked example

Problem: y varies directly with x, and y = 21 when x = 3. Write the equation and find y when x = 10.

  1. k = 21 ÷ 3 = 7, so y = 7x.
  2. When x = 10: y = 7 × 10 = 70.

Answer: y = 7x; y = 70

🎯 Practice

  1. Is y = 5x a direct variation? If so, what is k?

    Answer: Yes, k = 5 (for example, x = 2 gives y = 10, and 10 ÷ 2 = 5)

  2. Is y = 2x + 3 a direct variation?

    Answer: No, its graph does not pass through the origin (when x = 0, y = 3).

  3. y varies directly with x, and y = 12 when x = 4. Write the equation.

    Answer: y = 3x

  4. y varies directly with x, and y = −10 when x = 2.5. Find y when x = 6.

    Answer: y = −24 (k = −4)

  5. The distance d a car travels varies directly with time t. It goes 165 miles in 3 hours. Write an equation.

    Answer: d = 55t
