6.1 Percents and Decimals

💡 The big idea

Percent means "per hundred," so 35% is 35 out of 100. To write a percent as a decimal, divide by 100, which shifts the decimal point 2 places left: 35% = 0.35. To write a decimal as a percent, multiply by 100, which shifts the decimal point 2 places right: 0.8 = 80%. Percents can be bigger than 100% (like 250% = 2.5) or smaller than 1% (like 0.4% = 0.004).

📝 Key vocabulary

  • percent: a ratio out of 100
  • decimal: a number written using place value with a decimal point

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Write 7.5% as a decimal and 1.6 as a percent.

  1. 7.5% ÷ 100 → 0.075
  2. 1.6 × 100 → 160%

Answer: 0.075 and 160%

    6.2 Comparing and Ordering Fractions, Decimals, and Percents

    💡 The big idea

    To compare fractions, decimals, and percents, write them all in the same form first. Decimals are usually easiest: 3/5 = 0.6, 58% = 0.58, and 0.62 stays 0.62. Then compare the decimals place by place or put them on a number line. Writing everything the same way makes ordering a list quick and avoids mistakes.

    📝 Key vocabulary

    • equivalent forms: different ways of writing the same number, like 1/4, 0.25, and 25%
    • order: arrange from least to greatest or greatest to least

    ✏️ Worked example

    Problem: Order 3/5, 58%, and 0.62 from least to greatest.

    1. 3/5 = 0.6 and 58% = 0.58.
    2. Compare: 0.58 < 0.6 < 0.62.

    Answer: 58%, 3/5, 0.62

      6.3 The Percent Proportion

      💡 The big idea

      The percent proportion connects a part, a whole, and a percent: part/whole = percent/100. The whole (also called the base) is the amount you are taking a percent of, and the part is a piece of it. You can use the proportion to find any one of the three when you know the other two. For example, to find what percent 12 is of 48, solve 12/48 = p/100 to get p = 25.

      📝 Key vocabulary

      • percent proportion: part/whole = percent/100
      • whole (base): the total amount
      • part: a portion of the whole

      ✏️ Worked example

      Problem: What percent of 48 is 12?

      1. 12/48 = p/100
      2. p = 12 × 100 ÷ 48 = 25

      Answer: 25%

        6.4 The Percent Equation

        💡 The big idea

        The percent equation is a shortcut for percent problems: part = percent × whole. Always write the percent as a decimal or fraction before you multiply. For example, 15% of 60 is 0.15 × 60 = 9. If you know the part and the percent, divide to find the whole.

        📝 Key vocabulary

        • percent equation: a = p × w (part = percent × whole)

        ✏️ Worked example

        Problem: 27 is 60% of what number?

        1. 27 = 0.6 × w
        2. w = 27 ÷ 0.6 = 45

        Answer: 45

          6.5 Percents of Increase and Decrease

          💡 The big idea

          The percent of change tells how much a quantity changed compared with where it started. Use percent of change = (amount of change) ÷ (original amount). If the new amount is bigger, it is a percent of increase; if it is smaller, it is a percent of decrease. Percent error works the same way: compare the size of the mistake to the actual amount.

          📝 Key vocabulary

          • percent of change: change ÷ original, as a percent
          • percent of increase: percent of change when the amount goes up
          • percent of decrease: percent of change when the amount goes down
          • percent error: |estimate − actual| ÷ actual, as a percent

          ✏️ Worked example

          Problem: A plant grows from 20 cm to 26 cm. What is the percent of increase?

          1. Change = 26 − 20 = 6 cm.
          2. 6 ÷ 20 = 0.3 = 30%

          Answer: 30% increase

            6.6 Discounts and Markups

            💡 The big idea

            A discount is a decrease in price, so the sale price = original price − discount. A markup is an increase a store adds to what it paid, so the selling price = cost + markup. A quick trick: a 25% discount means you pay 75% of the price, and a 40% markup means the price is 140% of the cost. Sales tax works like a markup too.

            📝 Key vocabulary

            • discount: an amount taken off the price
            • markup: an amount added to the cost
            • sale price: the price after a discount
            • selling price: the price after a markup

            ✏️ Worked example

            Problem: A $48 jacket is 25% off. What is the sale price?

            1. You pay 100% − 25% = 75%.
            2. 0.75 × 48 = 36

            Answer: $36

              6.7 Simple Interest

              💡 The big idea

              Interest is money paid for using someone else's money: a bank pays you interest on savings, and you pay interest on a loan. Simple interest uses the formula I = Prt, where P is the principal (starting amount), r is the yearly interest rate written as a decimal, and t is the time in years. The total balance is the principal plus the interest. Remember to change months into years, so 6 months is 0.5 year.

              📝 Key vocabulary

              • principal: the amount saved or borrowed
              • interest rate: the percent of the principal paid per year
              • simple interest: I = Prt

              ✏️ Worked example

              Problem: You deposit $500 at 3% simple interest for 4 years. How much interest do you earn?

              1. I = Prt = 500 × 0.03 × 4
              2. I = 60

              Answer: $60
