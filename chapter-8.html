8.1 Circles and Circumference

💡 The big idea

A circle is all the points that are the same distance from a center. The radius goes from the center to the circle, and the diameter goes all the way across through the center, so d = 2r. The distance around a circle is its circumference, C = πd = 2πr. The number π (pi) is about 3.14, and it is the same for every circle because C ÷ d is always π.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • radius: distance from the center to the circle
  • diameter: distance across the circle through the center (2 × radius)
  • circumference: distance around a circle
  • pi (π): circumference ÷ diameter, about 3.14

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the circumference of a circle with radius 5 cm. Use 3.14 for π.

  1. C = 2πr = 2 × 3.14 × 5
  2. C = 31.4

Answer: about 31.4 cm

🎯 Practice

  1. A circle has a diameter of 18 in. What is its radius?

    Answer: 9 in.

  2. Find the circumference of a circle with diameter 10 m. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 31.4 m

  3. Find the circumference of a circle with radius 7 ft. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 43.96 ft

  4. A bike wheel has a circumference of 62.8 inches. What is its diameter? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: 20 inches

  5. A circular track has a radius of 50 m. About how far do you run in 3 laps? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 942 m

8.2 Perimeters of Composite Figures

💡 The big idea

A composite figure is made of two or more simpler shapes, like a rectangle with a half circle on one end. Its perimeter is the distance around the outside only, so do not count the lines where the shapes are joined. For a curved part, find the fraction of the circle's circumference you need, such as half for a semicircle. Then add up all the outside pieces.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • composite figure: a shape made of simpler shapes
  • perimeter: distance around a figure
  • semicircle: half of a circle

✏️ Worked example

Problem: A figure is a 10 m by 6 m rectangle with a semicircle on one 6 m side. Find the perimeter. Use 3.14 for π.

  1. Straight sides on the outside: 10 + 10 + 6 = 26 m.
  2. Semicircle with diameter 6: (3.14 × 6) ÷ 2 = 9.42 m.
  3. Total: 26 + 9.42 = 35.42 m

Answer: about 35.42 m

🎯 Practice

  1. A shape is a square with 5 cm sides plus a triangle with two 5 cm sides sitting on top. What is the perimeter?

    Answer: 25 cm (5 outside sides of 5 cm)

  2. Find the perimeter of a semicircle with diameter 8 in., including the straight edge. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 20.56 in.

  3. A track is a 100 m by 60 m rectangle with semicircles on both 60 m ends (the 60 m ends are not part of the track). Find the length of the track. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 388.4 m

  4. An L-shape has sides 8, 3, 5, 4, 3, and 7 ft. What is its perimeter?

    Answer: 30 ft

  5. A quarter circle has a radius of 4 m. Find its perimeter (the curve plus the two radii). Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 14.28 m

8.3 Areas of Circles

💡 The big idea

The area of a circle is the amount of space inside it, measured in square units. The formula is A = πr², which means π times the radius times the radius. If you are given the diameter, divide it by 2 to get the radius first. For half of a circle, find the full area and then divide by 2.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • area: the space inside a shape, in square units
  • r² (r squared): r × r

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the area of a circle with diameter 12 ft. Use 3.14 for π.

  1. r = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 ft.
  2. A = 3.14 × 6 × 6 = 113.04

Answer: about 113.04 ft²

🎯 Practice

  1. Find the area of a circle with radius 3 cm. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 28.26 cm²

  2. Find the area of a circle with diameter 20 in. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 314 in²

  3. Find the area of a semicircle with radius 4 m. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 25.12 m²

  4. A circular rug has a radius of 2.5 ft. What is its area? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 19.625 ft²

  5. A circle has an area of 78.5 cm². What is its radius? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: 5 cm (78.5 ÷ 3.14 = 25, and 5 × 5 = 25)

8.4 Areas of Composite Figures

💡 The big idea

For the area of a composite figure, break the shape into shapes you know, like rectangles, triangles, and parts of circles. Find the area of each piece, then add them together. Sometimes it is easier to subtract instead, like finding the area of a big rectangle and taking away a hole. Area is always measured in square units.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • composite figure: a shape made of simpler shapes
  • area of a triangle: A = 1/2 × base × height
  • area of a rectangle: A = length × width

✏️ Worked example

Problem: A figure is a 10 m by 6 m rectangle with a semicircle (diameter 6 m) on one end. Find the area. Use 3.14 for π.

  1. Rectangle: 10 × 6 = 60 m².
  2. Semicircle: r = 3, so 3.14 × 3 × 3 ÷ 2 = 14.13 m².
  3. Total: 60 + 14.13 = 74.13 m²

Answer: about 74.13 m²

🎯 Practice

  1. A house shape is a 8 ft by 6 ft rectangle with a triangle on top (base 8 ft, height 3 ft). Find the total area.

    Answer: 60 ft²

  2. A 12 in. by 12 in. square has a circle with radius 2 in. cut out. Find the remaining area. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 131.44 in²

  3. An L-shape is made from a 10 cm by 4 cm rectangle and a 3 cm by 5 cm rectangle that do not overlap. Find the area.

    Answer: 55 cm²

  4. A window is a 4 ft by 3 ft rectangle with a semicircle of diameter 4 ft on top. Find the area. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 18.28 ft²

  5. A circular garden of radius 5 m has a circular fountain of radius 1 m in the middle. What area is left for plants? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 75.36 m²
