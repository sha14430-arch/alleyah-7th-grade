✅ Must-know rules and formulas

16.1 Exponents

  • aⁿ means a multiplied by itself n times. (−2)⁴ = 16 but −2⁴ = −16.
  • Order of operations: exponents before × and ÷.

16.2 Product of Powers Property

  • Product of Powers: aᵐ · aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ. Power of a Power: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ. Power of a Product: (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ.

16.3 Quotient of Powers Property

  • Quotient of Powers: aᵐ / aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (a ≠ 0).

16.4 Zero and Negative Exponents

  • a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0). a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.

16.5 Reading Scientific Notation

  • Scientific notation: c × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ c < 10. Positive n → big number (move decimal right); negative n → small number (move left).

16.6 Writing Scientific Notation

  • To write in scientific notation, move the decimal so one nonzero digit is left of it; count the moves for n.

16.7 Operations in Scientific Notation

  • Multiply: multiply the c’s, add exponents. Divide: divide the c’s, subtract exponents. Add/subtract: make the exponents match first. Then adjust so 1 ≤ c < 10.

⚠️ Common mistakes and how to avoid them
  • Mistake: Multiplying the base by the exponent (2³ = 6).
    Avoid it: 2³ = 2 · 2 · 2 = 8.
  • Mistake: Multiplying exponents when you should add (x²·x³ = x⁶).
    Avoid it: Same base multiplied → ADD exponents: x⁵.
  • Mistake: Thinking a⁰ = 0 or a negative exponent makes a negative number.
    Avoid it: a⁰ = 1; 2⁻³ = 1/8 (positive).
  • Mistake: First factor outside 1–10 (12 × 10⁴).
    Avoid it: Rewrite: 1.2 × 10⁵.
  • Mistake: Moving the decimal the wrong direction.
    Avoid it: Big numbers → positive exponent; small numbers (less than 1) → negative exponent.

16.1 Exponents

💡 The big idea

A power is a short way to write repeated multiplication: 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. The base (2) is the number being multiplied, and the exponent (5) tells how many times it is used as a factor. Parentheses matter with negative bases: (−3)² = 9, but −3² = −(3 × 3) = −9. In the order of operations, do exponents right after parentheses.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • power: a product of repeated factors, like 2⁵
  • base: the factor being repeated
  • exponent: how many times the base is a factor

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Evaluate 4 + 3 × 2³.

  1. Exponent first: 2³ = 8.
  2. Multiply: 3 × 8 = 24.
  3. Add: 4 + 24 = 28

Answer: 28

    16.2 Product of Powers Property

    💡 The big idea

    When you multiply powers with the same base, keep the base and add the exponents: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ. When you raise a power to a power, multiply the exponents: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ. And when you raise a product to a power, raise each factor to that power: (ab)ᵐ = aᵐbᵐ. These rules come from simply counting how many factors there are.

    📝 Key vocabulary

    • Product of Powers Property: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
    • Power of a Power Property: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
    • Power of a Product Property: (ab)ᵐ = aᵐbᵐ

    ✏️ Worked example

    Problem: Simplify 3⁴ × 3² and (x³)⁵.

    1. Same base, add exponents: 3⁴⁺² = 3⁶.
    2. Power of a power, multiply: x³ˣ⁵ = x¹⁵.

    Answer: 3⁶ (which is 729) and x¹⁵

      16.3 Quotient of Powers Property

      💡 The big idea

      When you divide powers with the same base, keep the base and subtract the exponents: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (as long as a is not 0). This works because the matching factors on top and bottom cancel each other out. For example, 2⁶ ÷ 2⁴ = 2² = 4. You can combine this rule with the Product of Powers Property in longer problems.

      📝 Key vocabulary

      • Quotient of Powers Property: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
      • quotient: the answer to a division problem

      ✏️ Worked example

      Problem: Simplify (7⁵ × 7³) ÷ 7⁶.

      1. Top: 7⁵ × 7³ = 7⁸.
      2. Divide: 7⁸⁻⁶ = 7².
      3. 7² = 49

      Answer: 7² = 49

        16.4 Zero and Negative Exponents

        💡 The big idea

        Any nonzero number raised to the zero exponent equals 1: a⁰ = 1. A negative exponent means "one over": a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ, so 2⁻³ = 1/8. These definitions keep the exponent rules working, since 5³ ÷ 5³ = 5⁰ must equal 1. Negative powers of 10 are used for small decimals: 10⁻² = 0.01.

        📝 Key vocabulary

        • zero exponent: a⁰ = 1 when a ≠ 0
        • negative exponent: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ

        ✏️ Worked example

        Problem: Evaluate 4⁻² and (−9)⁰.

        1. 4⁻² = 1/4² = 1/16.
        2. Any nonzero number to the 0 power is 1.

        Answer: 1/16 and 1

          16.5 Reading Scientific Notation

          💡 The big idea

          Scientific notation is a short way to write very large or very small numbers as a × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer. To change it to standard form, move the decimal point n places: right if n is positive and left if n is negative. For example, 3.2 × 10⁴ = 32,000 and 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.00032. To compare numbers in scientific notation, compare the exponents first, then the front numbers.

          📝 Key vocabulary

          • scientific notation: a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10
          • standard form: the usual way of writing a number

          ✏️ Worked example

          Problem: Write 6.05 × 10⁵ and 4.1 × 10⁻³ in standard form.

          1. Move the decimal 5 places right: 605,000.
          2. Move the decimal 3 places left: 0.0041.

          Answer: 605,000 and 0.0041

            16.6 Writing Scientific Notation

            💡 The big idea

            To write a number in scientific notation, slide the decimal point until just one nonzero digit sits in front of it. Count how many places you moved it; that count becomes the exponent. Numbers 10 or greater get a positive exponent, and numbers between 0 and 1 get a negative exponent. For example, 52,000 = 5.2 × 10⁴ and 0.0007 = 7 × 10⁻⁴.

            📝 Key vocabulary

            • scientific notation: a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10
            • exponent of 10: how many places the decimal point moves

            ✏️ Worked example

            Problem: Write 0.000306 in scientific notation.

            1. Move the decimal 4 places right to get 3.06.
            2. The number is less than 1, so the exponent is negative.

            Answer: 3.06 × 10⁻⁴

              16.7 Operations in Scientific Notation

              💡 The big idea

              To multiply numbers in scientific notation, multiply the front numbers and add the exponents of 10. To divide, divide the front numbers and subtract the exponents. To add or subtract, first make the powers of 10 match, then add or subtract the front numbers. Finally, adjust the answer if the front number is not between 1 and 10.

              📝 Key vocabulary

              • Product of Powers Property: used to multiply powers of 10
              • Quotient of Powers Property: used to divide powers of 10

              ✏️ Worked example

              Problem: Find (3 × 10⁴) × (5 × 10²).

              1. Multiply front numbers: 3 × 5 = 15.
              2. Add exponents: 10⁴⁺² = 10⁶.
              3. 15 × 10⁶ = 1.5 × 10⁷

              Answer: 1.5 × 10⁷
