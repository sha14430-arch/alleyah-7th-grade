15.1 Volumes of Cylinders

💡 The big idea

The volume of a cylinder is the area of its circular base times its height: V = Bh = πr²h. It works just like a prism, because you are stacking circle-shaped layers. Remember to use the radius, so if you know the diameter, divide it by 2 first. Volume is measured in cubic units, like cm³ or ft³.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • cylinder: a solid with two congruent circular bases
  • V = πr²h: volume of a cylinder
  • cubic units: units for volume

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 3 in. and height 10 in. Use 3.14 for π.

  1. V = πr²h = 3.14 × 3² × 10
  2. = 3.14 × 9 × 10 = 282.6

Answer: about 282.6 in³

🎯 Practice

  1. Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 2 m and height 5 m. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 62.8 m³

  2. Find the volume of a cylinder with diameter 10 cm and height 4 cm. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 314 cm³

  3. A can is 12 cm tall with a radius of 4 cm. How much does it hold? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 602.88 cm³

  4. A cylinder has volume 141.3 ft³ and radius 3 ft. What is its height? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: 5 ft

  5. A cylinder has radius 1 in. and height 7 in. Find its volume in terms of π.

    Answer: 7π in³

15.2 Volumes of Cones

💡 The big idea

A cone has one circular base and comes to a point at the top. A cone fills only 1/3 of a cylinder that has a matching base and height, so its volume is V = (1/3)πr²h. Just like a pyramid, the height is measured straight up from the base to the tip. If you know the volume, you can work backward to find a missing height or radius.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • cone: a solid with one circular base and a vertex
  • V = (1/3)πr²h: volume of a cone

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the volume of a cone with radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. Use 3.14 for π.

  1. V = (1/3) × 3.14 × 3² × 4
  2. = (1/3) × 3.14 × 36 = 3.14 × 12 = 37.68

Answer: about 37.68 cm³

🎯 Practice

  1. Find the volume of a cone with radius 6 in. and height 5 in. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 188.4 in³

  2. A cylinder holds 300 cm³. How much does a cone with a matching base and height hold?

    Answer: 100 cm³

  3. Find the volume of a cone with diameter 6 m and height 10 m. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 94.2 m³

  4. A cone has volume 12π ft³ and radius 3 ft. What is its height?

    Answer: 4 ft

  5. An ice cream cone has radius 1 in. and height 6 in. How much ice cream fits inside (level with the top)? Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 6.28 in³

15.3 Volumes of Spheres

💡 The big idea

A sphere is a ball shape: every point on its surface is the same distance (the radius) from the center. Its volume is V = (4/3)πr³. A sphere fills exactly two-thirds of the cylinder that fits snugly around it. Since the radius is cubed, doubling the radius makes the volume 8 times as big.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • sphere: a perfectly round 3-D shape like a ball
  • V = (4/3)πr³: volume of a sphere
  • hemisphere: half of a sphere

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the volume of a sphere with radius 3 ft. Use 3.14 for π.

  1. V = (4/3) × 3.14 × 3³
  2. = (4/3) × 3.14 × 27 = 3.14 × 36 = 113.04

Answer: about 113.04 ft³

🎯 Practice

  1. Find the volume of a sphere with radius 6 cm in terms of π.

    Answer: 288π cm³

  2. Find the volume of a sphere with diameter 6 in. Use 3.14 for π.

    Answer: about 113.04 in³

  3. Find the volume of a hemisphere with radius 3 m in terms of π.

    Answer: 18π m³

  4. A sphere has volume 36π cm³. What is its radius?

    Answer: 3 cm (r³ = 27)

  5. If you double the radius of a sphere, how many times larger is the volume?

    Answer: 8 times

15.4 Surface Areas and Volumes of Similar Solids

💡 The big idea

Similar solids have the same shape, and all their corresponding lengths are proportional with scale factor k. If the scale factor is k, the ratio of their surface areas is k², because area is two-dimensional. The ratio of their volumes is k³, because volume is three-dimensional. So a model that is twice as tall in every direction needs 4 times the paint and holds 8 times as much.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • similar solids: solids of the same shape with proportional dimensions
  • ratio of surface areas: k²
  • ratio of volumes: k³

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Two similar cylinders have heights 4 cm and 12 cm. The small one has volume 20 cm³. Find the large one's volume.

  1. Scale factor k = 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
  2. Volume ratio is 3³ = 27.
  3. 20 × 27 = 540

Answer: 540 cm³

🎯 Practice

  1. Two cubes have edges 2 in. and 5 in. Are they similar? What is the scale factor?

    Answer: Yes, all cubes are similar; the scale factor is 5/2 (2.5).

  2. Two similar pyramids have a scale factor of 2. The small one has surface area 30 ft². Find the large one's surface area.

    Answer: 120 ft²

  3. Two similar boxes have a scale factor of 3. The small one has volume 10 in³. Find the large one's volume.

    Answer: 270 in³

  4. Cylinder A is 3 cm by 5 cm (radius by height). Cylinder B is 6 cm by 10 cm. Are they similar?

    Answer: Yes, 6/3 = 10/5 = 2.

  5. Two similar spheres have volumes 8 m³ and 125 m³. What is the scale factor from small to large?

    Answer: 5/2 (2.5)
