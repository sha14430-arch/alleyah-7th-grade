12.1 Parallel Lines and Transversals

💡 The big idea

A transversal is a line that crosses two or more other lines. When it crosses two parallel lines, it makes eight angles, and many of them are congruent. Corresponding angles (in the same position at each crossing) are congruent, and so are alternate interior angles (between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal) and alternate exterior angles (outside the lines, on opposite sides). Any two angles that form a straight line are supplementary, so they add to 180°.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • transversal: a line crossing two or more other lines
  • corresponding angles: angles in matching positions
  • alternate interior angles: inside the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal
  • alternate exterior angles: outside the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal

✏️ Worked example

Problem: A transversal crosses two parallel lines. One angle is 110°. Find its corresponding angle and the angle next to it on the same line.

  1. Corresponding angles are congruent: 110°.
  2. Angles on a straight line are supplementary: 180° − 110° = 70°.

Answer: 110° and 70°

🎯 Practice

  1. A transversal crosses two parallel lines, and one angle is 58°. What is its alternate interior angle?

    Answer: 58°

  2. Using the 58° angle above, what is the angle that forms a straight line with it?

    Answer: 122°

  3. Corresponding angles measure (4x)° and 100°. Find x.

    Answer: x = 25

  4. Alternate exterior angles measure (3x + 15)° and 75°. Find x.

    Answer: x = 20

  5. A transversal crosses two parallel lines. How many of the 8 angles are congruent to a 40° angle (including itself)?

    Answer: 4 (the other 4 angles measure 140°)

12.2 Angles of Triangles

💡 The big idea

The interior angles of a triangle always add up to 180°. If you extend one side of a triangle, you form an exterior angle outside the triangle. The measure of an exterior angle equals the sum of the two nonadjacent interior angles (the two inside angles that are not next to it). You can write and solve equations with these facts to find missing angles.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • interior angles: the angles inside a polygon
  • exterior angle: an angle formed by one side and the extension of a neighboring side
  • nonadjacent interior angles: the two inside angles not touching the exterior angle

✏️ Worked example

Problem: A triangle has interior angles 45° and 60°. Find the third interior angle and the exterior angle next to it.

  1. Third angle: 180 − 45 − 60 = 75°.
  2. Exterior angle: 45 + 60 = 105° (also 180 − 75 = 105°).

Answer: 75° and 105°

🎯 Practice

  1. Two interior angles of a triangle are 52° and 71°. Find the third.

    Answer: 57°

  2. An exterior angle of a triangle is 130°. One nonadjacent interior angle is 50°. Find the other.

    Answer: 80°

  3. A triangle has angles x°, (x + 10)°, and (2x − 10)°. Find x.

    Answer: x = 45

  4. An exterior angle measures (5x)° and the two nonadjacent interior angles are 40° and 60°. Find x.

    Answer: x = 20

  5. In a right triangle, one acute angle is twice the other. Find both acute angles.

    Answer: 30° and 60°

12.3 Angles of Polygons

💡 The big idea

A polygon is a closed figure made of straight sides. You can split a polygon with n sides into n − 2 triangles, so the sum of its interior angles is S = (n − 2) × 180°. In a regular polygon, all sides and angles are equal, so each angle is S ÷ n. The exterior angles of any convex polygon (one at each vertex) always add up to 360°.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • polygon: a closed figure with straight sides
  • regular polygon: a polygon with all sides and angles congruent
  • convex polygon: a polygon with no angles pointing inward

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon and the measure of each angle of a regular hexagon.

  1. n = 6, so S = (6 − 2) × 180 = 720°.
  2. Each angle: 720 ÷ 6 = 120°.

Answer: 720°; 120° each

🎯 Practice

  1. Find the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon.

    Answer: 540°

  2. Find the sum of the interior angles of a decagon (10 sides).

    Answer: 1,440°

  3. Find each interior angle of a regular octagon.

    Answer: 135°

  4. Find each exterior angle of a regular pentagon.

    Answer: 72°

  5. A polygon has an interior angle sum of 1,260°. How many sides does it have?

    Answer: 9 sides

12.4 Using Similar Triangles

💡 The big idea

If two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar; this is called Angle-Angle Similarity. Similar triangles have proportional sides, so you can set up a proportion to find a missing length. This lets you do indirect measurement, finding heights you cannot measure directly. For example, a tree and a person standing in the sun make similar triangles with their shadows.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • Angle-Angle (AA) Similarity: two pairs of congruent angles means the triangles are similar
  • indirect measurement: using similar figures to find a length you cannot measure directly

✏️ Worked example

Problem: A 5-foot person casts a 4-foot shadow. At the same time, a flagpole casts a 16-foot shadow. How tall is the flagpole?

  1. The triangles are similar, so 5/4 = h/16.
  2. h = 16 × 5 ÷ 4 = 20

Answer: 20 feet

🎯 Practice

  1. Triangle 1 has angles 40° and 65°. Triangle 2 has angles 40° and 75°. Are they similar?

    Answer: Yes. The third angle of triangle 1 is 75°, so both have 40° and 75°.

  2. Triangle 1 has angles 30° and 80°. Triangle 2 has angles 30° and 60°. Are they similar?

    Answer: No. Triangle 1 has angles 30°, 80°, 70°, but triangle 2 has 30°, 60°, 90°.

  3. A 6-foot fence post casts a 9-foot shadow. A building casts a 45-foot shadow. How tall is the building?

    Answer: 30 feet

  4. Similar triangles have sides 3, 4, 5 and 9, 12, x. Find x.

    Answer: x = 15

  5. A 1.5 m stick has a 2 m shadow. A tree has a 12 m shadow. How tall is the tree?

    Answer: 9 m
