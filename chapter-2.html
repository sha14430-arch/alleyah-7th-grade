✅ Must-know rules and formulas

2.1 Rational Numbers

  • A rational number can be written as a/b, where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0. Integers, fractions, terminating and repeating decimals are all rational.
  • Fraction → decimal: divide the numerator by the denominator (3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375).
  • A decimal terminates if it ends (0.25); it repeats if a block of digits repeats forever (2/3 = 0.666… = 0.6 with a bar over the 6).
  • In simplest form, a fraction makes a terminating decimal only if its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.
  • Mixed number → decimal: keep the whole number and convert only the fraction (−2 1/4 = −2.25).
  • Comparing: change everything to decimals (or a common denominator). On a number line, the number farther right is greater, so −0.5 > −0.75.

2.2 Adding Rational Numbers

  • Same signs: add the absolute values and keep the sign (−1/4 + (−1/2) = −3/4).
  • Different signs: subtract the absolute values and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value (−2.5 + 1.2 = −1.3).
  • To add fractions, use a common denominator first; add numerators only, never denominators.
  • Line up the decimal points when adding decimals.
  • Opposites add to 0 (Additive Inverse Property): 3/5 + (−3/5) = 0.

2.3 Subtracting Rational Numbers

  • Subtracting = adding the opposite: a − b = a + (−b). So 1/2 − (−1/4) = 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4.
  • Distance between two numbers on a number line = |a − b|.
  • With mixed numbers, rename (borrow 1 whole) if the fraction you subtract is bigger, or change both to improper fractions.

2.4 Multiplying and Dividing Rational Numbers

  • Sign rules: same signs → positive answer; different signs → negative answer (for both × and ÷).
  • An even number of negative factors → positive product; an odd number → negative product.
  • Multiply fractions: numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator; simplify (cross-cancel) first if you can.
  • Divide fractions: Keep–Change–Flip. Keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second (use its reciprocal): 2/3 ÷ (−4/5) = 2/3 × (−5/4) = −5/6.
  • Change mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing (1 1/2 = 3/2).
  • Multiplying decimals: count the total decimal places in the factors. Dividing by a decimal: move the decimal point in both numbers so the divisor is a whole number.
  • You cannot divide by 0, and 0 has no reciprocal.

⚠️ Common mistakes and how to avoid them
  • Mistake: Getting the sign wrong when multiplying or dividing negatives.
    Avoid it: Decide the sign FIRST (same signs → +, different → −), write it down, then do the arithmetic. Count negatives when there are 3 or more factors.
  • Mistake: Forgetting Keep–Change–Flip, or flipping the wrong fraction.
    Avoid it: Only the SECOND number (the divisor) gets flipped. Write the × step out before multiplying.
  • Mistake: Subtracting a negative as if it were subtracting a positive (5 − (−2) = 3 ✗).
    Avoid it: Rewrite every subtraction as adding the opposite: 5 − (−2) = 5 + 2 = 7.
  • Mistake: Adding denominators (1/4 + 1/3 = 2/7 ✗).
    Avoid it: Find a common denominator: 3/12 + 4/12 = 7/12. Denominators stay the same when you add.
  • Mistake: Dividing in the wrong order when converting (3/8 → 8 ÷ 3 ✗).
    Avoid it: Top ÷ bottom: numerator divided by denominator. Check: a proper fraction must give a decimal less than 1.
  • Mistake: Stopping a repeating decimal too early or putting the bar in the wrong place (1/6 = 0.16 with the bar over both digits ✗).
    Avoid it: Divide until you see the remainder repeat. 1/6 = 0.1666…, so the bar goes over the 6 only.
  • Mistake: Ordering negative numbers backwards (thinking −0.8 > −0.3).
    Avoid it: Picture a number line: the number closer to 0 on the negative side is greater, so −0.3 > −0.8.
  • Mistake: Treating a negative mixed number as two separate signs (−2 1/2 = −2 + 1/2 ✗).
    Avoid it: The negative applies to the whole number: −2 1/2 = −(2 + 1/2) = −5/2 = −2.5.
  • Mistake: Not simplifying the final fraction.
    Avoid it: Always reduce: divide numerator and denominator by their GCF (6/8 = 3/4).

2.1 Rational Numbers

💡 The big idea

A rational number is any number you can write as a fraction a/b, where a and b are integers and b is not 0. Every rational number can be written as a decimal that either stops (a terminating decimal, like 3/8 = 0.375) or repeats a pattern forever (a repeating decimal, like 2/3 = 0.666…, written 0.6 with a bar over the 6). To change a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator. Writing numbers as decimals makes them easy to compare and put in order on a number line.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • rational number: a number that can be written as a/b with integers a and b, b ≠ 0
  • terminating decimal: a decimal that ends
  • repeating decimal: a decimal with a block of digits that repeats forever

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Write −7/20 as a decimal.

  1. Divide: 7 ÷ 20 = 0.35.
  2. The fraction is negative, so the decimal is negative.
  3. Answer: −0.35

Answer: −0.35

    2.2 Adding Rational Numbers

    💡 The big idea

    You add rational numbers (fractions, mixed numbers, and decimals) with the same sign rules you used for integers. For fractions, first rewrite them with a common denominator, then add the numerators. With decimals, line up the decimal points. If the signs are different, subtract the absolute values and keep the sign of the number that is farther from zero.

    📝 Key vocabulary

    • common denominator: a shared denominator, often the least common multiple of the denominators
    • rational number: a number that can be written as a fraction of integers

    ✏️ Worked example

    Problem: Find −2/3 + 1/4.

    1. Common denominator 12: −8/12 + 3/12.
    2. −8 + 3 = −5.
    3. Answer: −5/12

    Answer: −5/12

      2.3 Subtracting Rational Numbers

      💡 The big idea

      Just like with integers, you subtract a rational number by adding its opposite: a − b = a + (−b). Rewrite fractions with a common denominator first, or line up decimals. To find how far apart two numbers are on a number line (their distance), subtract them and take the absolute value: |a − b|. This is handy for questions like "how far apart are these two temperatures?"

      📝 Key vocabulary

      • opposite: the same number with the other sign
      • distance between two numbers: |a − b|, which is never negative

      ✏️ Worked example

      Problem: Find 1/2 − 7/8.

      1. Common denominator 8: 4/8 − 7/8.
      2. 4 − 7 = −3.
      3. Answer: −3/8

      Answer: −3/8

        2.4 Multiplying and Dividing Rational Numbers

        💡 The big idea

        To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal (flip it). The sign rules are the same as for integers: same signs give a positive answer, different signs give a negative answer. Change mixed numbers to improper fractions before you multiply or divide.

        📝 Key vocabulary

        • reciprocal: a number flipped over; a number times its reciprocal is 1
        • improper fraction: a fraction whose numerator is at least as big as its denominator

        ✏️ Worked example

        Problem: Find −3/4 ÷ 9/10.

        1. Multiply by the reciprocal: −3/4 × 10/9.
        2. = −30/36 = −5/6.

        Answer: −5/6
