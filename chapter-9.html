9.1 Surface Areas of Prisms

💡 The big idea

A prism is a 3-D figure with two congruent, parallel bases that are polygons, and rectangles for its other faces. The surface area is the total area of all its faces, like the amount of wrapping paper needed to cover a box. Drawing a net (the figure unfolded flat) helps you see every face. For a rectangular prism, S = 2ℓw + 2ℓh + 2wh.

📝 Key vocabulary

  • prism: a solid with two parallel congruent polygon bases
  • surface area: the total area of all the faces
  • net: a flat pattern that folds into a solid
  • lateral surface area: the area of the sides only, not the bases

✏️ Worked example

Problem: Find the surface area of a box that is 6 in. long, 4 in. wide, and 3 in. tall.

  1. 2ℓw = 2(6)(4) = 48
  2. 2ℓh = 2(6)(3) = 36 and 2wh = 2(4)(3) = 24
  3. S = 48 + 36 + 24 = 108

Answer: 108 in²

    9.2 Surface Areas of Pyramids

    💡 The big idea

    A pyramid has one polygon base and triangle faces that meet at a single point at the top. The slant height is the height of each triangular face, measured along the face. The surface area is the area of the base plus the area of all the triangular lateral faces. For a regular pyramid (base is a regular polygon), all the triangles are congruent, so you can find one and multiply.

    📝 Key vocabulary

    • pyramid: a solid with one polygon base and triangular faces meeting at a vertex
    • slant height: the height of a triangular face
    • regular pyramid: a pyramid with a regular polygon (all sides and angles equal) as its base

    ✏️ Worked example

    Problem: A square pyramid has base edges of 6 m and a slant height of 5 m. Find its surface area.

    1. Base: 6 × 6 = 36 m².
    2. Each triangle: 1/2 × 6 × 5 = 15 m², and there are 4: 60 m².
    3. S = 36 + 60 = 96

    Answer: 96 m²

      9.3 Surface Areas of Cylinders

      💡 The big idea

      A cylinder has two congruent circle bases connected by a curved surface, like a soup can. If you unroll the curved part, it becomes a rectangle whose length is the circle's circumference and whose width is the cylinder's height. So the surface area is S = 2πr² + 2πrh: two circles plus the rectangle. The lateral surface area is just 2πrh, like the can's label.

      📝 Key vocabulary

      • cylinder: a solid with two parallel congruent circular bases
      • lateral surface area of a cylinder: 2πrh

      ✏️ Worked example

      Problem: Find the surface area of a cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 5 cm. Use 3.14 for π.

      1. Two bases: 2 × 3.14 × 2² = 25.12 cm².
      2. Side: 2 × 3.14 × 2 × 5 = 62.8 cm².
      3. S = 25.12 + 62.8 = 87.92

      Answer: about 87.92 cm²

        9.4 Volumes of Prisms

        💡 The big idea

        Volume is the amount of space inside a solid, measured in cubic units like cm³. For any prism, the volume is V = Bh, where B is the area of the base and h is the height of the prism. For a rectangular prism, that becomes V = ℓwh. Think of it as stacking layers: the base area tells how many cubes are in one layer, and the height tells how many layers there are.

        📝 Key vocabulary

        • volume: the space inside a solid
        • cubic units: units like in³ or m³ that measure volume
        • B (base area): the area of the prism's base

        ✏️ Worked example

        Problem: A triangular prism has a triangle base with base 6 cm and height 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long. Find its volume.

        1. B = 1/2 × 6 × 4 = 12 cm².
        2. V = Bh = 12 × 10 = 120

        Answer: 120 cm³

          9.5 Volumes of Pyramids

          💡 The big idea

          A pyramid holds exactly one-third as much as a prism with the same base and height. So the volume of a pyramid is V = (1/3)Bh, where B is the base area and h is the height. The height of a pyramid is the straight-up distance from the base to the top point, not the slant height along a face. If you pour sand from a pyramid into a matching prism, it takes three pyramids to fill it.

          📝 Key vocabulary

          • height of a pyramid: the straight-up distance from the base to the top point
          • V = (1/3)Bh: volume of a pyramid

          ✏️ Worked example

          Problem: A square pyramid has base edges of 6 ft and a height of 10 ft. Find its volume.

          1. B = 6 × 6 = 36 ft².
          2. V = 1/3 × 36 × 10 = 120

          Answer: 120 ft³

            Extension 9.5 Cross Sections of Three-Dimensional Figures

            💡 The big idea

            A cross section is the 2-D shape you see when you slice through a solid with a flat plane, like cutting a loaf of bread. Slicing a rectangular prism parallel to its base gives a rectangle the same size as the base. Slicing a cylinder parallel to its base gives a circle, but slicing it straight down through the middle gives a rectangle. Slicing a pyramid parallel to its base gives a smaller copy of the base shape.

            📝 Key vocabulary

            • cross section: the shape made where a plane cuts a solid
            • plane: a flat surface that goes on forever
            • parallel: running in the same direction and never meeting

            ✏️ Worked example

            Problem: A cylinder with radius 3 in. and height 10 in. is sliced straight down through the centers of both bases. Describe the cross section and find its area.

            1. The slice is a rectangle as wide as the diameter and as tall as the cylinder.
            2. Width = 2 × 3 = 6 in., height = 10 in.
            3. Area = 6 × 10 = 60 in²

            Answer: A 6 in. by 10 in. rectangle with area 60 in²
